Two months of travel ended this month: New Bedford, Woods Hole, Boston, the Bahamas, Toronto, Glasgow, Ulva, Barra, Liverpool, Southampton, London, Bristol, Pollica, Naples. Go where you're invited; to get invited, you have to show up. July was the hinge: we wrapped the Pollica project, flew home, and spent the back half of the month building the thing that invites people to come to us, a tall ship on the Salish Sea in September.

## Travel

- **Pollica, the in-between week**: the curated gastrodiplomacy program ended, and we had a car and time to ourselves; this turned out to be the hinge of the whole month. We drove down the coast and found resort owners and people who care about the ocean, who handed us paddle boards so we could get sensors in the water and start looking at the Posidonia before the bootcamp. We drove to Palinuro, named for the helmsman of Aeneas, the price Neptune took for delivering the fleet toward Latium. Watchtowers, storms coming in, the rain a relief from the sweltering heat, and far more hours than expected making sure every tool worked. Kate and I have been traveling off and on together for over ten years, and this was the most invited version of it we have ever done. Earning the invitation is what connected us to locals instead of making us a tour group.

- **Pollica, the Save the Ocean bootcamp**: the close of three weeks working with the [Future Food Institute](https://www.futurefoodinstitute.org/) on the coastal relationships of Pollica, in the Cilento about two hours south of Naples and the birthplace of the Mediterranean diet. The [bootcamp](https://climateshapers.org/en/bootcamp-save-the-ocean/) brought together students, municipalities, scientists, and regional policy makers around how the ocean, food, and governance relate: an illustrator, an ocean YouTuber, a Posidonia replanter working with One Ocean Foundation, the local oceanographer, and teenagers from all over Italy who genuinely wanted to work on ocean problems. Prep was temperature sensors, maps, and overlays, most of it making the tool show what we needed to see, in the way we needed to see it. Kate ran a brainstorming session with sticky notes: deconstruct, then construct, then find where the energy actually was. We went out for a 3am net haul with the fishermen and sat down with Mayor Stefano Pisani and Sara Roversi to map where the work goes next. The biggest thing I learned: we are standing in a graveyard of projects. Another science project that skips community, desire, and integration would be doomed to fail; Future Food has shown what community integration provides, and why it is necessary. The thing I watched Kate do all month that I want to steal: she doesn't decide the problem is solved and jump to building. She keeps exploring and stays interested.
- **The Blue Era talks**: Kate flew to the UK for more training and I stayed to finish the week with Future Food's Blue Era talk series: politicians, fishermen, thought leaders, academics, including some genuinely tense arguments between the politicians and the fishermen. The question I kept chewing on: when someone comes from far away to take in a moment, what gets left behind, and what should be?
- **Naples**: goodbyes after almost three weeks, then Naples alone, walking the city and working out how to talk about these ideas better. I needed the decompression and it was lovely.
- **The flight home**: Dublin to Seattle over Greenland, seated next to [Annie Bartholomew](https://anniebalaska.com/), an Alaskan folk musician and banjo player who travels to Irish and Nordic churches, helping restore them into music halls and getting communities to fold their old ballads back into the music they play now. Keeping music alive by giving it a room; a whole story on its own.
- **[PNW Climate Week](https://www.pnwclimateweek.org/)**: landed straight into it. Trivia night in Ballard with a table full of USAID alumni and the city's economic development people, a future of food event with Nice Industries, and wide-ranging conversations with [Washington Maritime Blue](https://maritimeblue.org/) and [Sound Experience](https://www.soundexp.org/).
- **Reassembly, back on the Sound**: the [Seattle Art Fair](https://seattleartfair.com/), a hike up Little Si, day sails out of Shilshole with friends, an evening out on *Petrichor*, the car fixed, and a reset day on Whidbey. The lesson from the first sail: a boat full of guests drifts toward charter mode, fun but shallow. Every one of those conversations deserved a follow-up, so now they get one. Also met a potential partner on sargassum and started researching collection efforts in Mexico and who funds that space.

## Projects

- **Island Lab: Pollica concluded**: the project with the Future Food Institute wrapped this month, and the output is a campaign and a protocol. The insight underneath both: Pollica, the Cilento, and Italy at large hold enormous pride in the land, the tomatoes, the olives, the mozzarella, and that pride does not extend to the water. The coast is not held the way the land is held. So the work has two halves. The campaign, run by the local food and climate week people, works on perception: the ocean deserves the pride the land already has. The protocols, run by Kate and me, are the action half, because pride alone is not action; if you love something you have to move in its direction. Side scan sonar, photogrammetry, and temperature sensors, a working answer to "what is the health of this area," with one load-bearing constraint: locals run it, not academics flown in from far away. What is the cheapest form of the tool that is still applicable? We are now applying for grants to implement more of these systems, aiming for a process that works, scales, and travels to other municipalities. A short write-up is up on [Island Lab](https://islandlab.dev/pollica.html); the full report is coming.
- **Island Lab: the Salish Voyage**: the big build of the month. September 17 to 20 we are taking the tall ship *Adventuress* ([Sound Experience](https://www.soundexp.org/)) out of Port Townsend for four days in the islands of the Strait of Juan de Fuca: an emergent gathering to create a chronicle of those who work, explore, create, and play on the Salish Sea. Everyone aboard learns the ropes and works the ship, and everyone contributes to the record. We capped climate week with a dinner for 25 aboard *Adventuress* to launch it, an hour of cooking and baking and then sauces and hors d'oeuvres, which I could not have done alone. The real lesson of the month is individuals, not groups: follow up with specific people, tell them why you would love to have them aboard, and ask what is preventing them from coming. This trip is the focus of the quarter. Details and applications at [islandlab.dev/salish26](https://islandlab.dev/salish26).
- **Island Lab: what a base looks like**: early scouting around Salt Spring Island on what a permanent Island Lab base would need: hosting residents and events, running experiments, and staying maintainable, with Ulva and the Pacific Whale Foundation model on Maui as reference points.
- **Bocas crossing**: an idea taking shape: two to four friends running the [Bocas kayak route](https://islandlab.dev/bocas-crossing.html) and exploring as we go.

## Tools

The first week home went to building, before the calendar filled back up. Three browser tools and the start of a fourth, each written up the day it shipped.

- **[Salish Migration Map]({{ '/salish/' | prepend: site.baseurl }})**: a map of the Salish Sea forage web and who moves through it, week by week: salmon runs, herring, both orca ecotypes, and the humpback, gray, and minke whales. The seasonal models are deterministic, calendar times place, so the map shows why the animals are where they are, and live sightings ride on top. Built in three days at the start of the month as groundwork for a whale alert that pings the phone when a sighting lands near the boat.
- **[Community Economic Simulator]({% post_url 2026-07-05-Community-Economic-Simulator %})**: run a small place through twenty turns of choices and see where the value it makes ends up staying, and who ends up owning it. A teaching tool for the Pollica work.
- **[The Facilitator]({% post_url 2026-07-05-The-Facilitator %})**: a game where you mediate between a rural community and an urban one through twenty turns of shocks, building trust and shared institutions before a crisis breaks them apart.
- **The Cilento map**: the drive map above, a single Leaflet file so the recap could show the coast instead of listing it.
- **[netmeter]({{ '/projects/netmeter/' | prepend: site.baseurl }})**: started on the last days of the month, because the boat runs on a phone hotspot and half a gigabyte was gone by 9am one morning with no receipt. A menu bar meter that says which app is spending the data. It grew a lot in August.

## Threads that kept showing up

- **Earned invitation over purchased access**: the month's best experiences all came from months of prior effort, not from booking something.
- **Pride precedes protocol, but protocol proves the pride**: the Pollica split is the cleanest version of this I have found.
- **Cheapest applicable tool, in local hands**: the sensor rig on a paddle board is the picture.
- **What gets left behind when outsiders come to take in a moment**: from the Blue Era arguments, and honestly from my own presence in Pollica.
- **The design process is the deliverable**: Kate's sticky-note session produced the campaign and protocol split. The method was the output.
