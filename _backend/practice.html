    The bum-ditty

    Everything here rides on one two-beat motion. Your hand only ever falls, and never picks up.

    bum
    Back of the index nail strikes one melody string, on the beat.
    dit
    Same nail brushes down across strings 3–2–1, sounding whatever chord you're holding.
    ty
    Thumb catches the 5th string on the way back. It is the drone, and it is free.

    Reading the tab

    i
    Index finger, striking down onto a single string.
    b
    Brush. The arrow shows the nail sweeping across three strings.
    T
    Thumb. On the bottom line that's the 5th-string drone; anywhere else it's a drop-thumb, and it will feel wrong for about a week.
    h
    Hammer-on. A brass arc back to the note it comes from. The right hand does nothing. A fretting finger slams down and sounds the note by itself.
    p
    Pull-off. The reverse: the fretting finger plucks sideways as it lifts, sounding the note underneath.
    s
    Slide. Keep the finger pressed and move it, so the pitch drags up into place.
    Wherever a brass letter replaces the i or T, your picking hand is idle for that note. Left hand only.

    The seven rungs

    Take one tune and walk up. Chord chart until the changes happen without you looking. Bum-ditty at a tempo where you never stumble. Skeleton keeps only the melody notes that land on a beat, so the tune appears without anything hard in it. Melody only is the tune bare, and you should be able to sing it.

    Melody and drone adds the 5th string and no brush, which is where it starts sounding like a banjo. Whole thing weaves the brush back in. Drop-thumb moves that off-beat thumb onto the 2nd string, and it will feel wrong for a fortnight.

    To work on a hard stretch, loop two measures: click one, then the next, and leave it at 45% until it is boring. Boring is the goal. Then nudge the tempo up 4 bpm at a time with the ] key.

    Melodies transcribed from public-domain sources: Boil Them Cabbage Down and When the Saints Go Marching In from the John Chambers ABC collection at MIT; Oh Susanna and Camptown Races from the Digital Tradition collection; Cripple Creek from Colin Hume's traditional-American set. All moved into the key of G and set in first position for open-G banjo. Rhythms are lightly simplified for a first pass.
