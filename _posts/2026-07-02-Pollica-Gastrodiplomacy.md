Last week Kate and I traveled to Pollica, in Cilento, to take part in the [Future Food Institute](https://www.futurefoodinstitute.org/)'s gathering for gastrodiplomacy. Pollica is a UNESCO emblematic community of the Mediterranean Diet, and it is hard to overstate how much living knowledge is held in this small place.

Over the week we met with Sara Roversi and her team, Peter Klosse and the Institute for Vital Gastronomy, Diane Dodd and [IGCAT](https://www.igcat.org/), colleagues from the [FAO](https://www.fao.org/), and a remarkable collection of chefs, teachers, scientists, and food-systems thinkers from around the world.

What stayed with us most was the time spent with the people who hold the system together day to day: farmers, fishermen, processors, and community connectors. They walked us through how the local diet and the local economy support one another, and they were honest about the strain. This is a region rich in culture and natural abundance, yet isolated, and watching its young people leave for opportunities elsewhere.

Building on this, Kate and I will be working in Pollica this month to develop environmental engagement systems with [Astraeus Ocean Systems](https://astraeusocean.com/) and [Island Lab](https://islandlab.dev/), to work with community members, not around them. The aim is to direct action toward grassroots community collaboration that can support and inspire the policy a region needs to cultivate its own ecosystem and economy on its own terms.

Thank you to everyone who hosted us. We are excited to keep doing the necessary, patient work of community engagement for resilience.
